Isotropic control

A simulated population with no preferred direction passed through the same reconstruction chain and binning. Any slope it returned would be instrumental; the paper uses it as the null for the sign of the slope, not for the level of the profile, which the simulation does not reproduce because it omits transport through the shield.

Markers: median elevation angle of core protons in 3° bins of inclination at the paper's selection, both phases, bins with at least 30 clusters, 68 % bootstrap intervals. The paper fits the shaded range only.

The elevation angle of any population organised by the field depends on how the field is tilted with respect to the sensor, and that tilt varies along the orbit. The paper therefore bins in the local magnetic inclination |I| and reads the slope of the profile rather than averaging over the orbit.

Three reference behaviours frame the reading. A population travelling along the field line arrives at 90° minus |I| from the sensor normal, a slope of minus one. An isotropic population gives a flat 46.7° after the reconstruction. A population at its mirror point moves perpendicular to the field; where the field is horizontal that plane of motion is vertical and the arrivals are steep, and as the field tips towards the vertical the plane tips towards the horizontal. With the gyration phase φ uniform, the vertical velocity component is cos φ cos |I| and the median angle is β = arccos(0.707 cos |I|): 45° at the equator, 69° at |I| = 60°, a slope near plus 0.57 over the fitted range.

The measured profile falls, so the organisation is of the field-aligned kind and not the mirroring kind, and it falls far less steeply than a purely field-aligned population would. The paper stops at the sign; the pitch-angle distribution behind it would need an angular response that includes the shield.

The detector does not observe continuously. It samples the field in exposures at a cadence of 64 s, about 1400 frames a day, with a nominal exposure of 25 s, so that acquisition covers 35 % of the elapsed time in the lower phase and 30 % in the higher one. Where the field is intense the exposure is shortened to keep the number of clusters in a frame within bounds: to a median of 1.35 s inside the anomaly at 613 km and 0.075 s at 1175 km, against 25 s elsewhere.

That is why the comparison is controlled on occupancy, the number of clusters in a frame, and not on rate: the rate is what the exposure control has already acted on. It is also why the core statistics are small. Over 73 and 96 days of live time, the core accumulated 1.2 and 3.6 hours.

The map shows the median exposure per frame along the orbit: the anomaly appears as a hole in the exposure, and the outer belt as a shallower one at high latitudes.

A particle crossing the 500 µm sensor deposits charge along its path. Projected onto the pixel matrix the track has a length L2D, counted in pixels of pitch p = 55 µm, and the elevation angle from the sensor normal follows from the geometry alone. The slider sets the measured quantity, the projected length; the angle is what the reconstruction reads from it.

β = arctan(p L2D / d)

The projected length is corrected for charge sharing by subtracting 2.5 times the lateral spread of the charge before it enters the formula. Two instrumental limits follow. Below about 25° the whole range is encoded in fewer than five pixels, and the residual length reflects the diffusion of the charge rather than the track; this reconstruction floor lies at 11.3° and 10.8° for protons in the two phases. At the other end the longest track that fits the 128 pixel read-out corresponds to 85.9°, and steeper tracks pile up at that ceiling.

What is not claimed

  • A calibrated slope: the magnitude follows the cluster-width limit with no plateau, and the width of the guard band behaves the same way on the core side.
  • A pitch-angle distribution: the sign separates a field-organised population from an unorganised one; the underlying distribution would need an angular response that includes transport through the shield.
  • A difference between the anomaly and its surroundings: the difference of medians varies by an order of magnitude with the occupancy threshold and is not reported.
  • Electrons: multiple Coulomb scattering in the 5 mm aluminium shield exceeds the angular scale of interest for the low-energy electrons that dominate the transmitted population.
  • Heavy ions: their reconstruction floor, above 50°, exceeds the range of interest; a heavy-ion signature reported in a preliminary analysis is instrumental.
  • The sense of travel: the cluster axis is an axis, not a vector, so the east-west asymmetry of the trapped flux cannot be tested with frame-mode data.
  • A contrast between the two altitudes: at 1175 km no definition of a region outside the anomaly is both uncontaminated and well populated.

Data and citation

Data on this page

Everything drawn here is a precomputed aggregate: medians, counts and fitted slopes over the grid of selections, exported from the analysis pipeline together with the paper's own numbers. No cluster-level data leave the pipeline; what the page shows is what it holds.
